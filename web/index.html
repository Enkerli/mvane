mvane yard

A firmware for the M-VAVE FM-1: chords from any scale under your fingers, a history that is always recording, and expressive sounds, light enough to play. Free and open source.

Download the latest release Source on GitHub Your FM-1 over USB

Install it with M-Flasher (below), after its backup of your FM-1.

What it does

Play chords on the white keys from any scale, their qualities latched on the black keys as on Coral Flower; everything you play is kept, so a phrase becomes a recording after the fact; play it through YARD, mvane yard's own light synth, or any of Clipper's 337 sounds, with MPE in and out.

Learn more
  • Chords: any pitch-class set (by its number, as in the Music Suite), qualities combined by pressing black keys together, single notes when none is latched, voicings (close, open, wide, pairs) led smoothly from one chord to the next, a separate bass.
  • Recordings: a history that never stops (and sleeps when you do); REC keeps the last phrase, eight slots keep recordings; a recording loops, follows the key and scale while it plays, and takes overdubs a pass at a time; to a DAW as one MIDI clip.
  • Sounds: families by use and by load (CHORDS, LEADS, HITS; EZ or HRD on the CPU); YARD (saw, pulse, triangle, noise, a filter that follows the note) and Clipper's engines, on both of the FM-1's cores; your own sounds saved on the panel and moved as files.
  • Expression: MPE out and in (pressure, slide, bend per note), a wind controller's breath, pressure routed on the sounds that ignored it.

Install

Download the .fwsc (above) and install it with M-Flasher, the tool M-VAVE itself pointed to on its forum: let it back up your FM-1's current firmware first, then install. This page does not install firmware from the browser, on purpose: a desktop flasher with a backup is the safe way back if anything goes wrong. M-Patcher works too.

Learn more
  • Releases are built from source on GitHub and signed with their provenance; each has the package (.fwsc), its checksum and a firmware.json.
  • Between releases, every change is built too (Actions, mvane-yard-0.12.N+commit), for trying what is new.
  • mvane yard is on The Firmware Hub's review list (submitted 2026-11-22).
  • In a DAW: the VIBE FM plugin's converter (TheFirmwareHub) offers an older version for now; a newer one is being worked out. On TheFirmwareHub itself: soon.

Play

White keys play the chord on each degree; black keys pick its quality; F#5 holds it. ALGO switches chords and single notes. PRESETS chooses the sound (a fast turn: the next family). REC keeps what you just played; PLAY plays it.

Learn more
  • HOME switches the knobs between PLAY (QUANT, KEY, SCALE, GLIDE) and SOUND (the sound's own); on SOUND, EDIT edits the sound (SAVE keeps it as yours) and GLO sets the output (PHONES MAX).
  • EDIT on PLAY: the voicing, how many voices, the bass, the quality bank; FX: a Clipper sound's sends; GLO: the play mode, USB audio, MPE out, the bend range; SEQ: the recording's slot and LOOP; SEL: the scale by its number.
  • The bottom line: the CPU's load, missed deadlines (XR), the sound's load (EZ, HRD).

Recordings

No recording saved in this slot yet: on the FM-1, SEQ or HOME to it, REC, then SAVE.

The FM-1 keeps eight recordings: SEQ and HOME pick the slot, SAVE keeps the recording there. Download all gives every slot at once, in a ZIP: each recording as MIDI, as MIDI with MPE channels, and as .mvt. A MIDI file sent to a slot becomes the recording PLAY plays in it: its notes, controllers, pressure and bend, all tracks merged (30,000 events at most). Download as MIDI puts everything on one channel, so a DAW makes one clip of it: each note's pressure as poly aftertouch (per-note pressure in Bitwig). MIDI with MPE channels keeps a channel a note, as played, which a DAW may split into a clip a channel.

What is a .mvt file?

mvane yard's own recording: what the FM-1 keeps and plays, exactly as you played it. Every event (a note, its pressure and bend, a knob move) is one 32-bit word: the milliseconds since the one before and a MIDI message, after a short header ("MVT1", a version, how many events, the length in milliseconds). Nothing is quantized or thinned, and the MIDI files are made from it, so keep the .mvt to keep the recording whole: it goes back to the FM-1 as it was. tools/mvt2mid.py in the repository converts it too, and core/rec.h describes it.

Your sounds and scales

YARD

Your own YARD sounds: on the FM-1, FX held and SAVE keeps the one playing. They come after YARD's nine on PRESETS.

#NameMonoToneReleaseGlide AttackDecayResDetuneSubPluckWavePWNoise Filter

Scales

#NameNumberFrom C

Clipper

#NameSoundReverbChanges (name=value)

Sending replaces the FM-1's own sounds and scales with these. Scales: a pitch-class set's number, 0 to 4095 (each semitone above the key a bit: the key 1, a semitone 2, a whole tone 4 ... the major seventh 2048; C major is 2741); the FM-1 lists them after its own, from LYDIAN to CHROMATIC, on SCALE and on SEL's layer (whose SAVE keeps the number set). A scale without a name shows as its number. YARD: values 0 to 127; GLIDE is kept with the sound, the FM-1 plays its own (the PLAY page's). Clipper: a factory sound and up to 14 of its parameters changed, by name or #number (BRITE, TIMBR, MOTN, SPACE: a HYBRID sound's M1..M4); on the FM-1, FX held and SAVE keeps a Clipper sound's macros, sends and reverb this way.
